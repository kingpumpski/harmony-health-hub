# Migration collision review: 20260912162000 and 20260912200000

Updated: 2026-11-25

## Scope and decision

Compared four duplicate-version candidates with the canonical hosted catalog. All inspection was read-only; no patient, audit, prescription, or payment row contents were queried.

Candidates:
- `20260912162000_clinical_change_audit_hardening.sql`
- `20260912162000_hims_workflow_completion.sql`
- `20260912200000_phase5_lab_catalogue_and_audit.sql`
- `20260912200000_prescription_payment_lifecycle_reconciliation.sql`

**Do not replay any candidate unchanged or declare either timestamp resolved.** Live schema objects have evolved, and the audit candidates introduce data duplication and potential sensitive-record over-collection.

## 1. Clinical audit trigger candidate 20260912162000

The candidate adds `audit_clinical_record_change()` to encounters, diagnoses, prescriptions, triage, laboratory catalogue, and insurance claims. Its metadata stores complete `OLD` and `NEW` row JSON.

The live schema instead has the shared `audit_operational_row_change()` trigger on these operational tables. It records the entity identifier, operation, changed-column names, and audit source, but does not copy complete old/new clinical row payloads into the generic audit stream. The candidate's `audit_clinical_record_change()` is not present in the inspected live catalog.

**Disposition:** preserve the leaner live row-audit design. Do not install a second generic clinical trigger that duplicates events or copies full clinical rows into audit metadata. If a specific regulation or workflow requires value-level change history, define an allowlist of fields, facility scope, access controls, retention, and redaction rather than serializing entire clinical rows.

## 2. Patient audit model has two competing tables

Both `patient_audit` and `patient_audit_log` exist in the live schema and each has a patient audit trigger:
- `trg_patient_audit` writes full old/new JSON to `patient_audit`.
- `t_patient_audit_changes` writes full old/new JSON to `patient_audit_log`.

This creates two audit stores for patient changes. Both tables carry potentially sensitive patient data in JSONB columns.

The observed RLS/policy model differs:
- `patient_audit` has an admin-only SELECT policy.
- `patient_audit_log` has a SELECT policy for all clinical staff or admins, without an observed facility predicate.
- The effective role grant listing reports broad table-level privileges for anon and authenticated on both tables. No anon policy was observed, so the grants do not prove anon row visibility; nevertheless they are excess privileges and should be revoked after trigger-owner and application-callsite checks.
- The live audit trigger functions are SECURITY DEFINER with `search_path = pg_catalog, public`.

**Priority repair/review:**
1. Establish which audit table is the canonical source and whether both are consumed by UI, reporting, exports, or external integrations.
2. Restrict patient audit reads to the patient's facility (and an explicitly approved platform audit role); clinical role membership alone must not grant global audit history.
3. Revoke direct client INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE and unnecessary anon grants on audit tables; preserve only the trigger/service pathways.
4. Decide whether full old/new JSON is necessary. Minimize fields, redact credentials/tokens and sensitive attributes, and establish retention/immutability controls.
5. Avoid dropping either table or trigger until consumers, retention, and historical records are reconciled.

## 3. Laboratory catalogue: parallel models exist

Both `lab_test_catalog` and `lab_test_catalogue` exist live, along with `lab_test_parameters`. The duplicate migrations introduce overlapping but non-identical laboratory catalogue concepts:
- One uses `lab_test_catalog` with a separate parameter model.
- The other uses `lab_test_catalogue` with a flat analyte/charge/reference model and links it to `lab_orders`.

Do not assume they are interchangeable or merge/drop either table during migration cleanup. Inspect foreign keys, UI callsites, RPCs, and data mappings before deciding whether one is canonical or the two represent distinct catalogues. Clinical reference intervals and interpretation remain laboratory-governed configuration, not automated treatment decisions.

## 4. Prescription payment lifecycle candidate is not represented by matching triggers

The candidate defines `sync_prescription_payment_status()` and `sync_prescription_dispensing_status()` triggers against `invoice_item_payments` and `pharmacy_dispensing_plans`, and includes a one-time paid-status reconciliation update.

The inspected live catalog contains neither named synchronization function nor matching prescription/payment trigger names. The live schema has both `invoice_item_payments` and `billing_item_payments`, and has a facility synchronization trigger on prescriptions. This is not enough to conclude the payment lifecycle is broken: newer service-order/payment RPCs may govern the process. It does mean the historical candidate is not proven applied and must not be replayed without reconciling the current canonical payment event model.

**Required next step:** trace current payment posting, invoice-item linkage, prescription status changes, dispensing-plan completion, reversals/refunds, and idempotency through the live RPCs and application callsites. Build a forward lifecycle only after defining one canonical payment source and handling partial payments, reversals, duplicate events, and cancelled/dispensed terminal states.

## 5. Other workflow foundations

The broader `20260912162000_hims_workflow_completion.sql` candidate introduces facility settings, service-order workflow, inpatient reviews, and related structures. This review did not certify that entire large migration line-by-line. Several later live models use different payment and service-order structures; do not infer that the candidate's full initial schema is the canonical live design. Continue with object-by-object signature, constraint, trigger, and callsite comparison.

## Verification boundaries

- Function definitions, trigger attachments, RLS flags, policies, effective table grants, and relevant column definitions were inspected.
- No patient, prescription, payment, audit, or lab catalogue row contents were read.
- No DDL, DML, grant changes, migration replay, or deployment occurred.
- No user-session authorization exploit test was run.

## Safety disposition

Keep all four historical candidates byte-for-byte. Do not bulk rename duplicate versions or create a replacement history from the current schema. Any approved repairs must be new forward-only migrations generated with the Supabase CLI, tested with pgTAP and disposable PostgreSQL 17/Supabase replay, and deployed only with explicit authorization.
